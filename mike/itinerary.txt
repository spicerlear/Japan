## How Your Specific Dates Reshape This Trip

A few important consequences of the May 9-14 / May 14-17 split:

**You miss the main Aoi Matsuri parade by one day.** It's on May 15. BUT — and this is genuinely good news — the festival has a series of pre-rituals throughout May, and **two of them fall during your Kyoto stay**:

- **May 12 (Tuesday) — Mikage Matsuri at Shimogamo Shrine, from 9am.** Priests welcome the festival's divine spirit down from Mt. Mikage in a small procession, followed by a sacred bugaku dance performed in Tadasu no Mori, the ancient forest at Shimogamo. This is one of Kyoto's most atmospheric, least-touristed sacred rituals. *This is essentially custom-designed for Sweizer's "meaningful cultural immersion" answer and for both of your crowd-allergy.* Highly recommend anchoring your May 12 around this.
- **May 14 (Thursday) — A smaller offering ritual at Shimogamo Shrine, from 10am.** This is your travel day to Tokyo, so probably skip unless your shinkansen is afternoon.

**Your Tokyo dates (May 14-17) overlap Sanja Matsuri (May 15-17) in Asakusa.** As mentioned, 2026 is the "Kagematsuri" (closed/ceremonial) year, so the famous mikoshi parade isn't happening for the public. There may be small ceremonial activity worth wandering into if you're in Asakusa, but don't plan around it.

**You're in Tokyo over a weekend (Sat May 16, Sun May 17).** This affects everything — popular spots will be more crowded, but the city's nightlife and street markets are at their best. We'll plan around it.

# KYOTO — May 9-14 (5 nights, 5 full days)

This is where the trip earns its name. With five days at a slow pace, you can actually *be* in Kyoto rather than checking it off. Here's a suggested anchor for each day — fill the rest with drift.

## Day-by-Day Anchors

### **Day 1 — Saturday, May 9: Arrival & Easing In**
Don't try to do anything ambitious. Drop bags, walk a neighborhood. Best low-key arrival anchor: **wander through Gion and Pontocho at dusk.** Lantern-lit alleys, the Kamogawa river, dinner at a small unpretentious place along the river. Bed early.

### **Day 2 — Sunday, May 10: Higashiyama at Dawn**
The big "we'd both regret missing this" day. **Set an alarm for 5:30am.** Walk the Higashiyama temple lanes — Sannenzaka, Ninenzaka, the path up to Kiyomizu-dera — while they're empty and beautiful. Wooden machiya houses, stone paths, lanterns, almost no one. By 9am the area floods with tourists; you'll be eating breakfast somewhere. Spend afternoon recovering — maybe Maruyama Park or a visit to **Chion-in temple** (huge, impressive, much quieter than Kiyomizu). Evening: rest.

> **Crowd note:** Sunday is the worst day for famous sites. Doing Higashiyama at sunrise is the only way it works on a weekend.

### **Day 3 — Monday, May 11: Arashiyama Day**
West side of Kyoto. Get to the **bamboo grove by 7am** — same crowd rule. After the bamboo, walk uphill to **Okochi Sanso Villa** (small fee, includes matcha and a sweet, almost no one knows about it — one of the most magical hidden gardens in Kyoto). Wander into Arashiyama town for lunch, see the river, maybe cross the **Togetsukyo Bridge.** Afternoon: take the small **Sagano Romantic Train** through the gorge if it's running, or visit the small temples uphill (Jojakko-ji, Gio-ji — moss garden — both gorgeous and quiet). Train back to central Kyoto for dinner.

### **Day 4 — Tuesday, May 12: ★ MIKAGE MATSURI + Northern Kyoto**
Today is the lock-in day. **Get to Shimogamo Shrine by 8:30am** for the Mikage Matsuri procession and bugaku dance starting at 9am. You'll watch sacred ritual in a 1,000-year-old forest with maybe a few dozen other people, most of them locals. This is the soul of Japan in a way the Heian parade on May 15 wouldn't quite be — slower, weirder, more sacred.

After: stroll through **Tadasu no Mori** (the forest itself is a thing — primeval feeling, wandering paths). Then north to the **Daitoku-ji temple complex** — a cluster of zen sub-temples with rock gardens, almost no tourists. Pick 2-3 sub-temples (Daisen-in, Koto-in, Ryogen-in are the famous ones). End the day at **Kamigamo Shrine**, the other end of the Aoi route, also quiet and lovely.

### **Day 5 — Wednesday, May 13: Day-Trip OR Workshop Day**

You said you wanted one day-trip and one hands-on workshop. Pick whichever sounds better and use this day for it.

**If day-trip — top 3 picks for your profile:**

- **NARA (1 hr by train):** Yes, the deer. But also Todai-ji's giant Buddha (genuinely awe-inspiring), Kasuga Taisha's lantern path through the forest, and **Naramachi** — a quiet old merchant district that scratches his "ordinary residential beauty" itch hard. Half or full day.
- **KURAMA & KIBUNE (45 min by train):** A small mountain village north of Kyoto. Hike from Kurama-dera temple over the mountain to Kibune (about 2 hours, gentle). Almost no foreign tourists. *Best fit if "nature and slow wandering" is your top priority.*
- **UJI (20 min by train):** The matcha capital of Japan. Tea fields, the stunning **Byodo-in** temple (the one on the ¥10 coin), and shops where you can grind your own matcha. Half-day. Easy and sensory.

**If workshop — top 5 picks:**

- **Indigo (aizome) dyeing** — make your own dyed cloth. Tactile and beautiful.
- **Knife forging or sharpening** at a Kyoto bladesmith — tailor-made for him.
- **Kyoto-style cooking class** in a small home setting (not a hotel) — Sweizer's "deep cultural immersion."
- **Kintsugi** (golden-joinery pottery repair) — meditative, you both make something together.
- **Tea ceremony at a real tea house** — *Camellia Garden* or *En* are well-regarded for being meaningful without being staged.

(If you want to do BOTH day-trip and workshop, do the workshop in a half-day and stack a half-day Uji or Nara visit. Most workshops are 2-3 hours.)

### **Day 6 — Thursday, May 14: Final Kyoto Morning, Travel to Tokyo**
Depending on shinkansen time: an early walk along the **Philosopher's Path** (canal-side, lined with trees, quiet, connects Ginkaku-ji to Nanzen-ji). Stop at **Nanzen-ji aqueduct** — gorgeous Meiji-era brick aqueduct running through a temple complex. Lunch near Kyoto Station, train to Tokyo.

## Anytime-You-Have-an-Hour Picks (Sprinkle Throughout)

- **Nishiki Market** — narrow covered food street. Early lunch on a weekday, eat your way through pickles, dashi rolls, fish cakes, matcha sweets. Sweizer's adventurous eating gets unleashed here.
- **Kaikado** (handmade tea caddies, 150+ years old), **Asahi-yaki** ceramics, the antique shops of Shinmonzen Street — for him.
- **Fushimi Inari at sunset.** The thousand red torii gates. By day it's selfies; at dusk it empties out and the lanterns flick on. Go up to Yotsutsuji (~30 min up) for the city view, then descend.
- **A real tea ceremony** if not done as your workshop.

## Splurge Meal in Kyoto

Kyoto invented kaiseki. For your one splurge, **do a lunch kaiseki** — same chef, same craft, half the price of dinner (often ¥8,000-15,000/person rather than ¥25,000+). Names worth researching: **Gion Karyo**, **Giro Giro Hitoshina** (informal, modern), or **Honke Owariya** for the simpler ancient soba experience that's been running since 1465.

## What to Skip Given Your Profile

- **Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion)** — controversial, but you stand in a herd and look at a building across a pond for 5 minutes. If you must go, opening time only.
- **Gion geisha-spotting tours** — touristy and uncomfortable. If you want geisha culture, do it via a meal or tea ceremony.
- **Bus tours** of any kind.

# TOKYO — May 14-17 (3 nights, ~2.5 full days)

You arrive Thursday afternoon, leave Sunday. That's effectively Friday + Saturday + Sunday morning of real time. Tokyo is enormous, so the move is **2-3 neighborhoods deep, not landmarks across the city.**

## Day-by-Day Anchors

### **Day 7 — Thursday, May 14: Arrive Tokyo, Easy Evening**
Drop bags. Don't be ambitious. Best easy first-night anchor: **a small izakaya in a residential neighborhood** — try **Sangenjaya** or **Yanaka** for a real-people feel rather than the touristy alleys. Eat, drink, walk home, sleep.

### **Day 8 — Friday, May 15: Yanaka + TeamLab Evening**
Morning: **Yanaka.** This is the lock-in for *him*. One of the few neighborhoods to survive WWII bombing — pre-war wooden houses, narrow lanes, neighborhood shrines, an old cemetery with cats and cherry trees. Wander Yanaka Ginza street, the cemetery, and **Nezu Shrine** (azaleas blooming around now, much quieter than the famous shrines). Lunch in Yanaka.

Afternoon: rest, or wander **Shinjuku Gyoen Garden** (best garden in the city, fresh greenery in May, peaceful on a weekday).

Evening: **TeamLab Planets or Borderless** — book a Friday evening slot well in advance (Friday night is busier than weekday evenings but you have limited days). Planets is more sensory and intimate (barefoot, water rooms). Borderless is more iconic. *Once-in-a-lifetime experience either way.*

### **Day 9 — Saturday, May 16: Senso-ji at Dawn + Kappabashi + Cultural Night**
**Up at 5:30am again** to do **Senso-ji at dawn.** The most famous temple in Tokyo. Mobbed by 9am, magical at 6am. Quietly walk Nakamise Street (closed up, no shops open yet — beautifully empty). Get the picture, get out. Breakfast in Asakusa.

Walk over to **Kappabashi Street** (between Asakusa and Ueno) — the "kitchen town" with handmade knives, ceramics, restaurant supply shops. *This is where he buys the perfect knife.* Small shops, real craftsmen, no crowds.

Afternoon: rest, or pick a craft/design neighborhood — **Shimokitazawa** (vintage, used books, indie cafés) or **Daikanyama / Nakameguro** (design shops, the Tsutaya bookstore, a quiet canal walk).

Evening: **Pick ONE of these for your cultural night** —
- **Jazz in a tiny basement bar.** Tokyo has the deepest jazz scene outside New York. *Cotton Club*, *Blue Note Tokyo* (bigger acts), or for the kissa vibe, **Eagle in Yotsuya** (listening only, no talking — meditative).
- **Kabuki at Kabukiza in Ginza.** Single-act tickets ¥1,000-2,500 so you don't commit to 4 hours. English audio guides available.
- **Meiji Jingu just before closing** (~5pm in May) — the forest path almost empty, profoundly peaceful. Pair with dinner in Harajuku/Omotesando after.

### **Day 10 — Sunday, May 17: Last Morning, Departure**
Depending on flight: a slow breakfast somewhere lovely, one final wander. **Daikanyama T-Site (Tsutaya bookstore)** is a beautiful Sunday morning anchor for design-lovers. Or **Hama-rikyu Garden** (waterside garden, traditional teahouse on a pond, quiet on Sunday morning). Train to airport.

## What to Skip Given Your Profile

- **Tokyo Tower / Skytree** — good views, but not what you're after
- **Akihabara** — pop culture isn't your top interest
- **Harajuku's main drag (Takeshita)** — one of the most crowded streets in the city; Saturday especially miserable
- **Shibuya Crossing on Friday/Saturday night** — chaos. If curious, do it Sunday morning.

## Crowd-Hack Calendar

- **Famous spots:** before 8am or after 5pm — *especially* on weekend days
- **Museums and gardens:** weekday mornings (you only have Friday)
- **Markets:** at opening (Tsukiji outer market by 7am if you go)
- **Restaurants on weekends:** book ahead or arrive at off-hours (5:30pm dinner, 1:30pm lunch)

# Suggested Day-Shape Template

Given your slow pace + crowd avoidance + flexible sleep schedules:

- **Early (6-9am):** ONE famous thing while it's still magical, OR sleep in
- **Mid-morning (9-12):** wander a neighborhood, find breakfast, drift
- **Lunch (12-2):** real meal, sit down, take time
- **Afternoon (2-5):** one intentional thing — workshop, museum, garden
- **Late afternoon (5-7):** another famous-spot-at-quiet-hours, or a long walk
- **Dinner (7-9):** small place, no reservation needed unless it's the splurge
- **Evening:** a bar, a night walk, or just collapse

Two anchors + drift is the magic ratio.
